# Exemplars — annotated moves

Form to imitate, not solutions to reuse. Read these excerpts only; do **not** open other features' discovery folders (their solutions anchor yours). Replace the illustrative examples with ones from your own domain over time — the *move* each teaches is what carries.

**Mechanism-level problem with a named case** — from an integration-PO discovery:
> "The channel treats an acknowledgement as a full replacement of the whole PO and implicitly rejects any line it doesn't see" — anchored to a real account's POs from that month.
The move: the statement teaches the reader something true about the world before any solution appears. A reader could reject the eventual design and still be glad they read the problem.

**A consequence-framed problem that generates a rejection test** — from an account-health discovery:
> "Operational issues compound in hours but surface to the operator in days; the cost is the preventable damage accumulating between onset and response, not the absence of a dashboard." → "Every element must measurably shrink onset-to-response time or get cut."
The move: framing the problem as a consequence produces a filter that every later design decision can be tested against. If your problem statement can't generate such a filter, it's describing a feature, not a problem.

**A decision that names its alternative and mechanism** — from a home-page discovery:
> "Fixed layout over conditional layouts — conditional layouts break spatial memory exactly when the user is stressed (debated; adaptive-prominence alternative rejected)."
The move: the decision names what lost, why in terms of a user mechanism, and the cost knowingly accepted — not a preference with "because" attached.

**A production number doing the problem's work** — from an integration health discovery:
> "83 purchase orders have closed without ever being accepted."
The move: one provenanced number outweighs paragraphs of assertion — and it hands the success metric over for free (that number, after).

**Caution, from the same corpus:** a discovery's #1 exploration bet once rested on an uncited claim about a competitor's scoring system that turned out to be wrong — and three "independent" ideation agents all echoed it because it sat in their shared input. External claims that decisions rest on get a source or [UNVERIFIED] + verification; convictions stay out of the material ideation agents read.
